Why This Matters

Mission Statement.

If you bought it, you own it — and owning something means being allowed to understand it, open it, and fix it.

Ownership Is the Whole Point

When you buy a device, it should be yours — not licensed, not leased, not quietly controlled by the company that sold it. Ownership without the right to repair is just a long-term rental with extra steps.

Repair Is Being Engineered Out on Purpose

Parts pairing, serialized components, glued-shut enclosures, and software locks aren't accidents. They're decisions that route every fix back to the manufacturer — or nudge you into buying new. That's not innovation; it's a toll booth on something you already paid for.

Independent Repair Keeps Things — and People — From Being Thrown Away

Access to parts, tools, schematics, and diagnostic software shouldn't be a privilege reserved for ‘authorized’ dealers. Independent shops and ordinary owners keep hardware alive, save people money, and keep working devices out of landfill.

This Isn't Piracy. It's Ownership.

Wanting the manual to the thing you paid for is not a threat to anyone. It's the baseline of a fair market — and the difference between a customer and a captive.

Standing on others’ shoulders. This mission is shaped by the advocates who turned repair into a movement — among them Louis Rossmann, whose case for ownership and against manufacturer repair monopolies influenced how many of us think about this fight.
