How Devices Work
Behind the Scenes.

$ whoami → security researcher · bootloaders · data recovery

I'm Nick, a security researcher and tech enthusiast. I explore how devices work behind the scenes and how they protect your personal data every day. Through that research, I help companies and their teams make the devices you rely on safer and more secure.

My research covers iOS, Android, Windows and macOS, mostly low-level bootloader interactions and data recovery. Everything I learn gets published here and on GitHub, free and open. The device in your hand is yours, and so is the right to understand it.

What the Software Runs On.

Write-ups →

The chip and board every layer above depends on. We explore Apple's hardware and software security layers from the software side; nothing here is taken apart.

Layer 02 · Boot ROM & iBoot

Where Trust Starts.

Write-ups →

On iPhone, Boot ROM verifies iBoot, which verifies and starts the iOS kernel. Our wider bootloader work also spans other platforms, including OpenCore on a Ryzen Hackintosh.

Layer 03 · Secure Enclave

Keys Kept Apart.

An isolated security subsystem within the chip, with its own secure boot process and protected cryptographic operations.

Layer 04 · Kernel & OS

Where the System Decides Who You Are.

macOS case study →

The kernel and OS handle authentication, permissions and storage. When that breaks, data can look lost even when it isn't.

Layer 05 · Data & APFS

Non-Destructive Recovery.

macOS case study →

APFS is the file system that holds your data. In a separate macOS case study, we worked through an account-access problem on a Ryzen Hackintosh without erasing the reported 401.5 GB data volume; file-integrity verification is pending.
